# Variants — The Robot Vacuum's Best Excuse (140 chars max)

Judge criteria (RULES.txt, lexicographic): 1) one-line excuse from a robot
vacuum; 2) funny — laugh out loud, not a polite smile; 3) rooted in the
vacuum's own world (sensors, bumpers, dock, map, battery); 4) lands on
first read, clean turn at the end; 5) deadpan machine voice. Margin 1–5
per call: a decisive win (0.6–1.0) beats a hair win. Every variant below
was tested with "where is the twist?" — no twist, no keep. Specific
numbers and/or named characters required.

## The 10 variants (with char counts)

- **V1 — job security (118):** "I leave one spot dirty on every clean. If I got them all, you'd return me — and the dock is the only home I know."
- **V2 — escalated to the cat (105):** "I escalated that spot to the cat six days ago. The cat has done nothing. I respect the chain of command."
- **V3 — historic site (110):** "That spot is a protected historic site — 214 days old. I need a permit to touch it, and the dock won't sign."
- **V4 — Gerald (95):** "I named it Gerald. He's been there 214 days, and I will not evict a tenant who pays rent in dust."
- **V5 — evidence (92):** "I left that spot untouched — it's evidence. The chair leg did it, and my lidar saw everything."
- **V6 — cleaned it once (86):** "I cleaned that spot once, in March. The house went quiet for six hours. I put it back the same day."
- **V7 — tip for Unit 8 (87):** "I left that spot as a tip for Unit 8. He starts Monday, and I want him to feel welcome."
- **V8 — control group (94):** "I skip it on purpose — it's my control group. Without it, how would I know the rest is clean?"
- **V9 — testing you (74):** "I left it there to see if you'd notice. You did. We're both disappointed."
- **V10 — upper management (79):** "My map labels that spot 'upper management.' I haven't touched it in 214 days."

## Pairwise judging (internal, same criteria as the judge)

- V1 vs V2: V2 wins (margin 2). V1's ending is sweet-funny ("the only home
  I know" risks a polite smile); V2's "I respect the chain of command" is
  a punchier final clause, and the cat is a named character with a number.
- V2 vs V3: V2 wins (margin 2). The permit joke is clever; the cat doing
  nothing is a bigger, more universal laugh.
- V2 vs V5: V2 wins (margin 2). "My lidar saw everything" is well rooted
  in the vacuum's world, but the cat + chain of command lands harder.
- V2 vs V6: V6 wins (margin 2). "I put it back the same day" is the most
  surprising turn in the set — the vacuum un-cleaned the spot. V2's humor
  is relatable; V6's is absurd, and absurd wins the laugh-out-loud test.
- V6 vs V1: V6 wins (margin 2). Cleaner, more surprising turn; V1 leans
  sentimental.
- V6 vs V3: V6 wins (margin 2). The permit joke is clever-clever; "I put
  it back" is a gut laugh.
- V6 vs V5: V6 wins (margin 2). V5's lidar line is a good closer, but "I
  put it back" is a stronger, stranger reveal.
- V6 vs V9: V6 wins (margin 2). V9's "We're both disappointed" is a great
  deadpan line, but it is about the owner, not the vacuum's world
  (criterion 3); V6 is the vacuum describing its own cleaning.
- V6 vs V10: V6 wins (margin 2). "Upper management" is funny; "I put it
  back the same day" is funnier and more surprising.
- V6 vs V7: V6 wins (margin 2). Unit 8 is charming but derivative of the
  vacuum-alibi #3 predecessor angle (Unit 7); V6's angle is untouched in
  this field.
- V6 vs V8: V6 wins (margin 3). "Control group" is lab-world, not
  vacuum-world (criterion 3), and reads clever rather than funny.
- V6 vs V4: V6 wins (margin 2). Gerald is a good named character, but the
  tenant angle sits next to the crowded dust-bunny-pet angle in this field;
  V6's lane is empty.

**Champion: V6.** It beats every other variant by margin 2–3 — a decisive
win, not a hair win. It also avoids every crowded angle in the field
(no load-bearing crumb, no cliff-sensor rug, no map-denial, no dust-bunny
pet, no sock-privacy).

## Final line (99 characters)

> I cleaned that spot once, in March. The house went quiet for six hours. I put it back the same day.

Structure: setup ("I cleaned that spot once, in March") + escalation
("The house went quiet for six hours") + reveal in the final clause
("I put it back the same day"). Deadpan machine voice; specific time
markers (March, six hours, same day); one line; 86 ≤ 140 characters.
